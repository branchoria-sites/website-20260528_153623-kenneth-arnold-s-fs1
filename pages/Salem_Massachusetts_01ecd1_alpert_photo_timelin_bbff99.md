## Introduction

The **Salem, Massachusetts photograph of 16 July 1952** stands as one of the more frequently discussed visual records from the early post‑war surge in reported unidentified aerial phenomena. At the centre of this episode is **Shell R. Alpert**, a young United States Coast Guard photographer whose sudden visual encounter with unusual lights outside his photo laboratory culminated in a single shutter click that has since been scrutinised by investigators, journalists, sceptics and UFO researchers alike. This page provides a focused, chronologically ordered timeline of Alpert’s sighting and photographic actions — from first noticing the lights through to the moment the image entered official investigative channels. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

## Morning in the Photo Laboratory

On the **morning of *16 July 1952***, at **approximately 09:35 AM**, Shell R. Alpert was on duty at the United States Coast Guard Air Station on Winter Island near Salem, Massachusetts. Employed as a professional photographer for the service, Alpert was attending to routine tasks in the photo laboratory — reportedly filing and cleaning camera equipment — when he experienced an unexpected visual event. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

According to Alpert’s later accounts, he had his *back toward the laboratory window* when something outside drew his attention. Upon turning slightly, he observed **four unusually bright lights** arranged in a loose *“V”‑shaped formation in the sky, apparently near the line of a nearby power‑plant’s smokestacks. These lights were described as “wavering” in brightness or intensity as he watched them. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

## Alpert, Flaherty, and the Reported Lights

After a few seconds observing the lights alone, Alpert **called out to his colleague, Hospitalman 1st Class Thomas E. Flaherty**, who was working elsewhere in the station, to come and look. When Flaherty arrived at the window, witnesses reported that the lights again brightened after a momentary dimming. It was at this brief second interval — as the lights re‑intensified — that Alpert seized the opportunity to take a photograph. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

Alpert swiftly reached for a camera he had nearby. Some accounts note he was uncertain in the moment whether the camera still contained film, as he had been cleaning it, but *fortunately it did*. With the camera in hand, Alpert exposed his film through the laboratory window screen, capturing **one image** of the four luminous points against the sky. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

Immediately after the shutter was released, Alpert and Flaherty reported that the lights **dimmed and disappeared suddenly**, in what one witness described as a “momentary flash”. The entire visual encounter — from first noticing the lights to their disappearance — lasted only the few minutes required to call for Flaherty and expose the frame. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

## From Shutter Click to Official Handling

The print that resulted from Alpert’s exposure was officially designated **Coast Guard Photo 5554**. Following standard procedure for unusual incidents involving potential unidentified aerial phenomena during that era, the photograph and related witness accounts were passed up through the Coast Guard chain of command. The picture was first handed to Alpert’s superior at the Salem station, then **flown to Coast Guard Headquarters in Washington, D.C.**, and eventually forwarded to the **U.S. Air Force** for examination under its formal UFO investigative programme, **Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book-5db429/' | relative_url }})**. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://theufodatabase.com/incidents/1952-salem-ufo-photo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1952 salem ufo photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Database1952 Salem UFO Photo | The UFO Database...</span></span></span>

In the ensuing review, Air Force analysts initially proposed a series of conventional [explanations]({{ 'explanations-093918/' | relative_url }}) — including the possibilities of double‑exposure artefacts or reflection effects — before ultimately categorising the case as **“unexplained”** within the Project [Blue Book files]({{ 'blue-book-5db429/' | relative_url }}). This classification meant that, based on the documentation available at the time, investigators did not identify a conventional source that could account for the photographed lights. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ufo - UFOs at close sight: Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 1952</span></span></span>

The photograph itself was subsequently archived and made accessible in official collections, including digitised holdings at the **Library of Congress** (where it is catalogued as a 1952 photographic print taken by Alpert through a window screen). Its presence in public repositories has ensured that the image continues to be referenced in historical overviews of the 1952 UFO wave. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.loc.gov/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressFirst daylight photo of - what are they? | Library of Congress...</span></span></span>
